# Cloudsprig Tea: test better preparation in four stores

This is entirely synthetic material. Cloudsprig Tea is fictional; all names, numbers and situations are invented.

## 1. Background: improve existing stores before expanding the approach

Cloudsprig Tea is a small chain with 8 tea-drink stores. Managers prepare ingredients based on experience and summarize inventory in a spreadsheet each evening. The operations team proposes an 8-week preparation and replenishment pilot in 4 stores. The other 4 stores would keep their current process. This decision covers the pilot only, not a chain-wide rollout.

## 2. Problem: waste and shortages occur together

All baseline figures below cover the 4 proposed pilot stores combined over one synthetic 30-day observation period. They do not describe all 8 stores.

| Metric | 30-day baseline | Pilot target |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Orders | 400 per store per day; 48,000 in total | Use 48,000 for budget estimates; assume no growth |
| Ingredient purchases | CNY 400,000 | Baseline only; purchases are not guaranteed to stay constant |
| Cost of discarded ingredients | CNY 36,000, or 9% of purchases | Waste rate at or below 6% |
| Orders requiring a substitution because an item was unavailable | 8%, or 3,840 orders | At or below 4% |
| Median drink wait during peak hours | 7 minutes | At or below 5 minutes |

The issue is not simply too little stock: popular ingredients run out in the afternoon while slower-moving ingredients are discarded at closing. Daily totals arrive too late to guide the next shift. The aim is to improve shortages, waste and waiting without increasing regular shift staffing.

## 3. Options: balance spending against speed of learning

These are one-time pilot budgets for the same 4 stores and 8 weeks. They exclude existing wages and routine ingredient purchases.

| Option | One-time budget | Approach | Main tradeoff |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A: Keep the daily report | CNY 0 | Managers continue adjusting by experience | No new spending, but no intraday replenishment information |
| B: Shared inventory board and replenishment rules | CNY 30,000 | Record stock by time of day, add afternoon reminders and adjust preparation weekly | Testable within 8 weeks, but depends on accurate store records |
| C: Custom forecasting system | CNY 90,000 | Integrate orders and stock, then develop automated forecasts | Integration may consume the full 8 weeks without validating outcomes |

Budgets for B and C are planning estimates for this exercise, not vendor quotes. None of the options has demonstrated the target results.

## 4. Recommendation: approve B, then decide whether to expand

Approve option B for 4 stores over 8 weeks, with a CNY 30,000 cap. Allocate CNY 12,000 to board setup, CNY 10,000 to training and workflow design, and CNY 8,000 to data reconciliation and evaluation. These total CNY 30,000. The existing operations lead would own the pilot.

At the baseline purchase level of CNY 400,000 per 30 days, reducing waste from 9% to 6% would bring discarded ingredient costs to CNY 24,000, a reduction of CNY 12,000. This is a target scenario at constant purchases, not realized savings or a promised return. It includes no additional-order revenue and is not extrapolated to the other 4 stores.

## 5. Plan: reach a continue-or-stop decision in 8 weeks

- Weeks 1–2: The operations lead reconciles the baseline and ingredient categories and configures the board. The 4 pilot store managers train their existing teams.
- Weeks 3–6: All 4 stores use intraday stock records and afternoon replenishment reminders. Operations and store managers review weekly, without increasing regular shift staffing.
- Weeks 7–8: The finance lead reconciles waste costs; operations evaluates substitutions and waiting. Compare daily metrics from the final two weeks against baseline daily metrics, keeping stores, opening hours and definitions consistent. Identify promotion-day effects separately.

Discuss rollout only if the final two weeks achieve all three targets: waste at or below 6%, substitution orders at or below 4%, and peak median wait at or below 5 minutes, without increased regular shift staffing. Otherwise explain the shortfall and recommend revising or stopping the approach; expansion is not automatic. Present the 30-day savings scenario separately from the two-week measured results.

## 6. Risks and the decision requested

- Missing records could misdirect replenishment. Store managers reconcile records at shift handover; operations spot-checks weekly.
- Promotions could distort demand. Flag promotion days and discuss them separately so demand swings are not mistaken for process improvements.
- Recording could slow service. Review waiting weekly; if it worsens, managers simplify the recording steps and retest.

Ask the general manager to approve the CNY 30,000 cap, 4 pilot stores and 8-week duration, assign the operations lead, and confirm finance participation in the week-8 evaluation. No chain-wide rollout budget is requested now; that requires a separate decision.
